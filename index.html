What is an Australian degree worth?

Choosing a university degree is one of the biggest financial decisions a young Australian makes. This story explores the data behind graduate starting salaries — which fields pay best, whether the university matters, and how gender and further study change the picture.

Diminishing returns on education

Before looking at fields, we need to look at the return on education itself. A bachelor's degree adds roughly $19k over the general workforce median. A master's adds another $24k.

But a PhD adds only $11k on top of a master's — despite taking three to four additional years of study. For most students, the return on a research degree is personal, not purely financial.

1 · The income landscape graduates enter

Australia is a wealthy country, but that wealth is not evenly distributed. Before we look at graduates, it helps to see the geographic context they are entering.

Where the money is

Sydney's North Shore and Eastern Suburbs sit at the top of the country, alongside Melbourne's Inner East. Meanwhile, regional areas, the Northern Territory, and western Tasmania anchor the bottom of the distribution.

Where inequality is worst

2 · Does the university matter?

The common assumption is that the prestigious Group of Eight universities produce the highest-paid graduates. The data suggests otherwise.

Employment rates by institution

Each bubble is a university, sized by its full-time employment rate. While the Group of Eight are the biggest names, several regional universities actually outperform them on employment outcomes.

3 · The field matters more than the university

Salaries have risen roughly 30% across every field since 2020, and the ranking of those fields has barely moved.

The stability is the finding

Over six years — including a pandemic and a cost-of-living crisis — no field has moved more than three places in the salary ranking. If you want the highest starting salary, the answer is stable and blunt: study Dentistry.

A heatmap of stability

This heatmap confirms the story: darker cells (higher salaries) remain in the same rows year after year. Field choice locks in your starting salary more than any other factor in this dataset.

4 · Gender and the pay gap

Treating all graduates in a field as identical hides an important variation: gender.

A gap in almost every field

Female graduates earn less than male graduates in nearly every field, even at the point of first employment. The gap is widest in Architecture and Communications (around 8–10%), and smallest in Nursing and Pharmacy, where award wages dominate.

This is crucial context for interpreting the "typical salary" of any discipline.

6 · The international student gap

A narrowing gap

International graduates have consistently earned less than domestic graduates at the same universities. But the gap peaked in 2022 and has narrowed since, likely reflecting a shift in the fields international students are choosing.

What does this mean for a student?

1. Field of study moves starting salaries by $40k. University choice moves them by $20k. If you can only optimise for one thing, optimise for the field.

2. Employment isn't automatic. Even in the most employable fields, 5–10% of graduates are still looking for full-time work four months after finishing.

3. The master's is the sweet spot. A coursework master's adds the biggest premium to your starting salary. A PhD adds the smallest.
