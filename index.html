What is an Australian degree worth?

Graduate starting salaries across fields of study, universities, and regions — built from the ABS regional income tables and the QILT Graduate Outcomes Survey.

The value of each extra year of study

A bachelor's degree adds roughly $19k over the general workforce median. A master's adds $24k more. A PhD adds only $11k on top — despite, typically, three to four additional years of study.

1 · The income landscape graduates enter isn't spread evenly

Australia's highest earners cluster in a handful of inner-city regions; the lowest cluster in remote and regional areas. Two maps establish the context for everything that follows.

Regional income, mapped

The darkest regions on this map sit more than double the median income of parts of regional Tasmania and northern NSW. But the story isn't just "rich coast, poor inland" — the most striking split is between a handful of inner-city SA4s and everything else.

Sydney's North Shore and Eastern Suburbs sit at the top of the country, alongside Melbourne's Inner East. Meanwhile the whole of the Northern Territory, regional South Australia, and western Tasmania anchor the bottom of the distribution.

Fig 02. Median personal income by SA4 region, 2022–23 (ABS Personal Income in Australia).

Where inequality is worst

Two regions can have the same number of earners and completely different income distributions. This map colours each region by its Gini coefficient — a standard measure of inequality, where 0 means everyone earns the same and 1 means one person takes everything.

Sydney's Eastern Suburbs tops the country at 0.63 — the top 1% of earners there take home nearly a fifth of all income. By contrast, Western Sydney — with a similar number of earners — sits in the palest band, close to the national median of 0.48.

Fig 03. Bubble size = number of earners. Colour = Gini coefficient (inequality). ABS 2022–23.

2 · Universities in regional Australia often out-earn the Group of Eight

The common assumption is that prestigious, big-city universities produce the highest-paid graduates. The data suggests otherwise — the University of New England, a regional campus in Armidale, has the highest undergraduate median in the country.

Geography of graduate outcomes

Each bubble is a university. Size = the proportion of its 2025 undergraduates who found full-time work within four months of finishing. The Group of Eight are the biggest names — but they are not the best performers.

Fig 04. Proportional symbol map of full-time employment rates by university. QILT GOS 2025.

Postgraduate salaries vary twice as much as undergraduate salaries

Undergraduate salaries cluster tightly between $67k and $87k across every Australian university. Postgraduate coursework salaries span $90k to $135k — nearly twice the spread. The choice of university matters more at postgraduate level than at undergraduate level.

3 · The field you choose matters more than the university you attend

Salaries have risen roughly 30% across every field since 2020, and the ranking of those fields has barely moved. Dentistry leads. Pharmacy trails.

Salary growth 2020–2025

Every one of the ten highest-paying fields gained over the period. Dentistry pulled furthest ahead — its 2025 median is 27% higher than the second-place field.

Fig 06. Slope chart. The ten highest-paying undergraduate fields in 2025, traced back to their 2020 starting salaries.

Field × year stability

Darker cells = higher salaries. The horizontal bands barely shift across six years — the ranking of fields is remarkably stable.

Fig 07. Heatmap of median undergraduate salary by field and year, 2020–2025. Fields sorted by 2025 salary.

The stability is the finding. Over six years — including a pandemic and a cost-of-living crisis — no field has moved more than three places in the salary ranking. Field choice locks in starting salary more than any other factor in this dataset.

4 · Beyond the headline number: gender and qualification level

Treating all graduates in a field as identical hides two important variations. Gender changes the picture, and so does how far you take your education.

A pay gap in almost every field

Female graduates earn less than male graduates in nearly every field, even at the point of first employment. The gap is widest in Architecture and Communications (around 8–10%), and smallest in Nursing and Pharmacy, where the workforce is highly feminised and award wages dominate.

The story isn't universal disparity — it's that the gap depends on the field. This matters for students interpreting the "typical salary" for their discipline.

5 · No field wins on everything at once

Which fields look balanced across multiple outcomes, and which fields lean heavily in one direction?

The salary–employment–job-match triangle

Each dot is a field. Position shows where its profile leans — toward salary strength, employment strength, or job-match strength. A dot near the centre is equally strong (or weak) on all three.

Pharmacy sits on the bottom edge: strongest employment profile, weakest salary. Creative Arts leans hard toward the job-match corner, reflecting the absence of salary and employment strength.

International graduates earn less — but the gap is narrowing

The international–domestic salary gap widened through 2022 and has narrowed since. Both groups' salaries grew steadily; the recent narrowing likely reflects a shift in the fields international students are choosing.

Three things a student can take from this data

1 · Interest first

There is no perfect field to study, fields that are strong on every outcome. Students should prioritise interest and aptitude first, and treat salary and employment as secondary considerations.

2 · Selection of University is important

The choice of university matters in employment and salary outcomes, especially at postgraduate level. Students should consider the university's location, reputation, and graduate outcomes when making their decision.

3 · Master's degrees offer the best return

Master's degrees are the only postgraduate qualification that consistently out-earn undergraduate degrees in terms of starting salaries. PhDs are the least rewarded in salary, despite the fact that it requires significant more time and financial investment.
