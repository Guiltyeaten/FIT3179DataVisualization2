What is an Australian degree worth?

An exploration of graduate starting salaries across Australian fields of study, universities, and regions — built from two public datasets: the ABS regional income tables and the QILT Graduate Outcomes Survey.

Choosing a degree is the biggest financial decision most Australians make in their twenties

Tuition, three to five years out of the workforce, and the opportunity cost of not earning while studying all add up. But the payoff isn't uniform: a Pharmacy graduate starts on roughly $62,600, while a Dentistry graduate starts on $103,100 — a 65% gap between two fields that both demand five years of study.

Every chart that follows uses median starting salaries — the middle graduate in each group, not an average distorted by a handful of extreme earners. That gives a more realistic picture of what a typical student can expect.

The income landscape graduates enter isn't spread evenly

Before looking at graduates specifically, it helps to see the wider geography. Australia's highest earners cluster in a handful of inner-city regions; the lowest cluster in remote and regional areas. The two maps below show regional income and the level of inequality within each region.

Median personal income by SA4 region, 2022–23 (ABS). The darkest areas sit more than double the median income of parts of regional Tasmania and northern NSW.

Bubble size = number of earners. Colour = Gini coefficient (a measure of inequality). Sydney's Eastern Suburbs has the highest inequality in the country.

Universities in regional Australia often out-earn the Group of Eight

The common assumption is that prestigious, big-city universities produce the highest-paid graduates. The data suggests otherwise. Regional universities like the University of New England, Charles Darwin, and Charles Sturt consistently sit near the top of undergraduate salary rankings.

Each bubble is a university at its real geographic location. Size = full-time employment rate of its 2025 undergraduates.

Each dot is a university. Horizontal position = median starting salary. Vertical jitter is for readability only.

The field you choose matters more than anything else in this dataset

Salaries have risen roughly 30% across every field since 2020, and the ranking of those fields has barely moved. If you want the highest starting salary in Australia, the answer is stable and blunt: study Dentistry. If you want the lowest, the answer is equally stable: Pharmacy, Communications, or Creative Arts.

Median undergraduate salary for all 21 broad fields, 2020–2025. Darker cells = higher salaries. The horizontal bands show the ranking is remarkably stable.

Two things complicate the story: gender and level of study

Measuring salaries as if all graduates in a field were identical hides two important variations. Gender changes the picture, and so does how far you take your education.

Male vs female median starting salary by broad field, undergraduate 2025. Sorted by the size of the gap.

A gender pay gap appears in almost every field — even at the point of first employment. It is widest in Architecture and Communications (around 8–10%) and smallest in Nursing and Pharmacy.

There are no perfect fields, each field has its trade-offs

The final charts step back from single numbers. They ask: if we could measure a field's profile across three outcomes at once, which fields look balanced, and which look lopsided?

Each dot is one field. Position shows where its profile leans — towards salary, employment, or job-match. A dot near the centre is equally strong on all three.
