Graduate starting salaries in Australia

Which degrees pay best, where in the country are graduates earning most, and how has the picture changed over the last five years? This visualisation explores salaries, employment and further study outcomes for Australian university graduates using the QILT Graduate Outcomes Survey and ABS regional income data.

1Where are graduates earning the most?

Median personal income varies sharply across Australia. The choropleth below maps income by SA4 region; the proportional symbol map shows the size and starting salary of each university; the tile grid gives a quick state-by-state ranking.

2Which fields pay best — and who wins?

Dentistry, medicine and engineering top the rankings, but rankings shift over time. The slope chart shows how the top ten fields moved between 2020 and 2025. The dumbbell chart exposes the gender gap, and the heatmap shows every field × year combination at a glance.

3Employment, further study and overqualification

Salary alone doesn't tell the whole story. The Marimekko chart shows how each field's graduates divide between full-time work, further study and other outcomes. The connected scatterplot tracks salary vs full-time employment over the last five years.

4How have salaries changed over time?

Domestic graduates earn consistently more than international graduates, but the gap narrows for postgraduate research degrees. The area chart shows the trajectory by citizenship, and the small multiples break down individual fields.

Data sources: QILT Graduate Outcomes Survey (2020–2025) via qilt.edu.au; ABS Data by Region (SA4) via abs.gov.au.
