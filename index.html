What is an Australian degree worth?

Graduate starting salaries across fields of study, universities, and regions — built from the ABS regional income tables and the QILT Graduate Outcomes Survey.

The value of each extra year of study

A bachelor's degree adds roughly $19k over the general workforce median. A master's adds $24k more. A PhD adds only $11k on top — despite, typically, three to four additional years of study.

1 · The income landscape graduates enter isn't spread evenly

Australia's highest earners cluster in a handful of inner-city regions; the lowest cluster in remote and regional areas. Two maps establish the context for everything that follows.

2 · Universities in regional Australia often out-earn the Group of Eight

The common assumption is that prestigious, big-city universities produce the highest-paid graduates. The data suggests otherwise — the University of New England, a regional campus in Armidale, has the highest undergraduate median in the country.

Postgraduate salaries vary twice as much

Undergraduate salaries cluster tightly between $67k and $87k across every Australian university. Postgraduate coursework salaries span $90k to $135k — nearly twice the spread.

Fig 04. Jitter dot plot. Each dot is a university. Horizontal position = median starting salary.

3 · The field you choose matters more than the university you attend

Salaries have risen roughly 30% across every field since 2020, and the ranking of those fields has barely moved. Dentistry leads. Pharmacy trails.

The stability is the finding. Over six years — including a pandemic and a cost-of-living crisis — no field has moved more than three places in the salary ranking. Field choice locks in starting salary more than any other factor in this dataset.

4 · Beyond the headline number: gender and qualification level

Treating all graduates in a field as identical hides two important variations. Gender changes the picture, and so does how far you take your education.

A pay gap in almost every field

Female graduates earn less than male graduates in nearly every field, even at the point of first employment. The gap is widest in Architecture and Communications (around 8–10%), and smallest in Nursing and Pharmacy, where the workforce is highly feminised and award wages dominate.

The story isn't universal disparity — it's that the gap depends on the field. This matters for students interpreting the "typical salary" for their discipline.

International graduates earn less — but the gap is narrowing

The international–domestic salary gap widened through 2022 and has narrowed since. Both groups' salaries grew steadily; the recent narrowing likely reflects a shift in the fields international students are choosing.

Three things a student can take from this data

1 · Field first

Field of study moves starting salaries by $40k. University choice moves them by $20k. Geography moves them by less. Optimise for the field.

2 · Employment isn't automatic

Even in the most employable fields, 5–10% of graduates are still looking for full-time work four months after finishing. In less-employable fields the figure is closer to 20%.

3 · The master's is the sweet spot

A coursework master's adds the biggest premium. A PhD adds the smallest. For most students, the return on a research degree is not financial.
